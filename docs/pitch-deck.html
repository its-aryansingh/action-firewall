The control gap

A model is not a payment principal.

Natural language can express intent. It cannot carry durable authority across cart edits, policy changes, retries, and provider timeouts.

1 · Agent

Prompt injection, hallucinated quantities, or ambiguous checkout language can alter a proposal.

2 · State

The cart and policy can change between preview and dispatch. A stale yes is unsafe.

3 · Effect

Retries and timeouts can duplicate an external action even when the database reserved once.

The missing primitive is exact, one-use authorization at the action boundary.

Audit + recovery

Every transition leaves evidence before the network

Authorization and dispatch-start events commit with their state transition. A denial never reaches the actuator; ambiguity remains reserved.

Three truths stay separate

  • Event log: attempts, denials, transitions.
  • Outstanding exposure: authorized, dispatching, issued, unknown.
  • Confirmed payment: only after authoritative evidence.

SQLite rejects audit-row updates and deletes. This is application append-only evidence, not a signed ledger.

A timeout after dispatch is not “nothing happened.” It becomes UNKNOWN, blocks redispatch, and preserves headroom.

Safe ambiguity beats optimistic retry.

Objections + limits

What this proves—and what it does not

“Rules engine with AI decoration?”

AI handles open-ended retrieval and cart operations. The payment boundary is deterministic because model creativity is not authority.

“Policy changes mid-checkout?”

Version and policy hash are revalidated at dispatch claim. A stale unclaimed grant is cancelled before I/O.

“Razorpay times out after accepting?”

The attempt becomes UNKNOWN, keeps headroom, and cannot auto-repeat. Reconciliation needs authoritative evidence.

“Identity or Vulcan integrated?”

No. Browser identity is an MVP stub and no private Vulcan API is assumed. Production needs authenticated principals and provider events.

Retry Budget stays secondary: reopen only with verified constraints and measured batch lift against fixed-schedule and oracle baselines.

One action is deliberate scope, not a claim of platform completeness.
